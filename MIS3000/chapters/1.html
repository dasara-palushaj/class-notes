Business Driven MIS​

Information Age: infinite quantities of facts available to anyone who uses a computer.

  • Everything around us collects information, even companies can use our information
  • Nearly every move you make is captured
    • Chinese loans & phone data; historical data, see who was approved, correlate to their technology; iPhone v. Android; short battery, lot of mistakes typing, less likely to pay
    • Walmart; collects info before tornados, stock up beforehand; strawberry poptarts, water, beer, batteries
    • Target; pregnancy pamphlets in mail to teen girl, father angry, she shopped for baby products
    • Google is now advertising company, no longer search engine

4 Components/Core Drivers of the Information Age:

  1. Data: Raw facts that describe the characteristics of an object or event.

    • Companies collect data all the time
    • GrizzlyID, first name, last name, email; anything entered into a form
    • Quantity ordered, customer first/last name, location

    Two Types of Data:

    1. Structured Data; defined type, length, and format (ex: SSN, address, DOB) [20%]

      2. - Human Generated (ex: filling out a form)

      - Machine Generated (ex: web log data, clickheat maps, point of sale data)

    2. Unstructured Data; no defined type, length, or format (ex: video/pic, email, text, audio recordings…etc) [80%]

      3. - Human Generated (ex: writing an email, sending a text message/tweet)

      - Machine Generated (ex: security camera recording a video, atmosphere data, satellite images, any smart device)

    Big Data: Collection of large complex data sets (structured and unstructured data) that cannot be analyzed using traditional database methods/tools.

    Snapshot: View of data at a particular point in time.

  2. Information: Data which is converted into a meaningful and useful context.

    • Information is as good as the person using it (a report can be interpreted differently by two managers)

    Variable: a piece of data that keeps changing (varies) (ex: stock price, item price)

    Static Information/Data: info/data that gets created once, and does not change (ex: sales tax 6%, stock symbol, GrizzlyID, SSN)

  3. Business Intelligence: Information collected from different sources that analyzes patterns, trends, and relationships for decision making (ex: find out the most popular product purchased along with a drink)

    4. *Business Intelligence = big picture, Business Analytics = subset within BI

    (Business) Analytics: Science of fact-based decision making. It allows you to transform data into insights for making better decisions.

    • Descriptive Analytics: use techniques that describe past performance and history. It produces a report for decision makers to help them make a decision. (ex: average, median, charts, graphs…etc)

      • “What happened?”

    • Predictive Analytics: use techniques that predict the future and identify patterns (ex: predict sales using past sales data, predict weather)

      • “What will happen?”

    • Prescriptive Analytics: use techniques using present and historical information to recommend the best decision that should be made by a business (ex: maximize profit by using past and current hours to predict the most efficient scheduling hours)

      • “What should we do?”

    Wastewater epidemiology - big data to analyze populations

    *Feed garbage data, receive garbage data (ex: biases in samples)

  4. Knowledge: Skill, experience and expertise, information and intelligence that creates a person’s intellectual resources.

    5. Two Types of Knowledge:

    1. Knowledge Workers: people valued for their skill to analyze and interpret information (ex: executives, managers, day-to-day workers)
    2. Knowledge Facilitators: people who help to harness knowledge; knowledge management systems

Management Information System (MIS): a business function that moves information about people, products, and processes across the organization to facilitate decision making and problem solving.

MIS Department Roles and Responsibilities:

Executives:

  • Chief Information Officer: oversees all uses of MIS and ensuring that MIS strategically aligns with business goals and objectives
  • Chief Knowledge Officer: responsible for collecting, maintaining, and distributing company knowledge
  • Chief Security Officer: ensures the security of business systems and developing strategies and safeguards against attacks by hackers and viruses
  • Chief Technology Officer: responsible for ensuring the speed, accuracy, availability, and reliability of the MIS
  • Chief Privacy Officer: ensures the ethical and legal use of information within a company
  • Chief Data Officer: responsible for determining the types of information the enterprise will capture, retain, analyze, and share

More executives are hired by companies with money, if not, usually has a CIO (multidisciplinary job)
